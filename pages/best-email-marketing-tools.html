The tools, one by one

Best for: Small businesses that want simple and affordable

MailerLite

MailerLite is the tool we'd hand to most small businesses first. The editor is clean, automations are easy to follow, and you get landing pages and sign-up forms included. It doesn't try to do everything, which is exactly why it's easy to stick with.

Watch out for: Fewer advanced automation and reporting features.

Best for: All-in-one marketing with webinars and courses

GetResponse

GetResponse goes beyond email. You get automation workflows, landing pages, and on higher plans, webinars and a course creator. It's a good fit if you'd otherwise pay for an email tool, a webinar tool and a course platform separately.

Watch out for: Prices climb as your list grows, and the best automation isn't on the entry plan.

Best for: Ecommerce stores (especially Shopify)

Klaviyo

Klaviyo is built around store data. It knows what each customer bought, browsed and abandoned, so flows like abandoned cart, browse abandonment and win-back get very specific. If you run an online store, it's the one to beat. If you don't, you're paying for features you won't use.

Watch out for: Gets expensive as your list grows, and it's overkill for non-store businesses.

Visit Klaviyo →

Best for: Advanced automation and CRM

ActiveCampaign

ActiveCampaign is the automation power tool. Multi-branch journeys, lead scoring, site tracking and a built-in sales CRM let you build almost any follow-up you can imagine. Expect a learning curve, and budget time to set it up properly.

Watch out for: A steeper learning curve, and costs add up across plans and add-ons.

Visit ActiveCampaign →

Best for: Email plus SMS and WhatsApp on a budget

Brevo

Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) bundles email, SMS, WhatsApp and transactional email. Its paid plans are priced by how many emails you send each month (Starter begins at 5,000 emails) rather than how many contacts you store, which can be a big saving if you have a large list you email occasionally.

Watch out for: Some advanced features sit on higher plans, SMS credits cost extra, and the free plan's 300-emails-a-day cap limits bigger sends.

Visit Brevo →

Best for: Beginners who want lots of integrations

Mailchimp

Mailchimp is the name most people know, and it connects with nearly everything. It's friendly for beginners and has solid templates. The catch is value: as your list grows, simpler tools like MailerLite often do the same job for less.

Watch out for: Pricing has crept up, and the free plan is limited.

Visit Mailchimp →

Best for: Creators selling digital products

Kit (formerly ConvertKit)

Kit uses tags and visual automations to send each subscriber the right sequence. Combined with built-in selling of digital products, that makes it a strong pick for creators who turn readers into customers.

Watch out for: Built for creators rather than stores or B2B.

Best for: Agencies that need email, SMS and CRM for clients

GoHighLevel

GoHighLevel isn't an email tool first. It's an agency platform with CRM, funnels, SMS, booking and email in one, with a separate workspace for each client. If you manage marketing for several clients, it can replace a whole stack. If you just need email, it's overkill.

Watch out for: A real learning curve, and more platform than you need if email is all you want.

How email tool pricing really works

Headline prices are almost meaningless on their own. Before you choose, look at:

  • What you're billed on. Most tools charge by the number of contacts. A few charge mainly by emails sent. A big list you email once a month can cost very different amounts on each.
  • The price at your next milestone. Check the cost at 5,000, 10,000 and 25,000 contacts. That's where the gaps between tools open up.
  • Which plan has the feature you actually need. Automation, A/B testing and abandoned-cart flows are often one tier up from the cheapest plan.
  • Add-ons. SMS credits, extra seats, dedicated IPs and transactional email are often billed separately.

Deliverability basics (whichever tool you pick)

The best tool in the world won't help if your emails land in spam. The basics are the same everywhere:

  1. Authenticate your domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Every tool on this list has a setup guide.
  2. Only email people who opted in, and make unsubscribing easy.
  3. Clean your list regularly. Remove bounces and people who haven't opened in months.
  4. Warm up a new sending domain by starting with your most engaged subscribers.
  5. Follow the rules where your subscribers live: CAN-SPAM in the US, GDPR and PECR in the EU and UK, CASL in Canada.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best email marketing tool in 2027?

There isn't one best tool for everyone. MailerLite is the easiest good-value pick for most small businesses. Online stores should look at Klaviyo, automation-heavy B2B teams at ActiveCampaign, and agencies at GoHighLevel.

Which email marketing tool is free?

MailerLite, Brevo, Klaviyo, Mailchimp and Kit all have free plans with limits on contacts, sends or features, and GetResponse keeps a limited free account after its 14-day trial. ActiveCampaign and GoHighLevel offer free trials instead. Check each tool's current limits.

Contacts-based or sends-based pricing: which is cheaper?

If you have a big list but email it rarely, pricing based on emails sent (like Brevo's) is often cheaper. If you email a smaller list often, contacts-based pricing can work out better. Price both at your real numbers.

Do I need SPF, DKIM and DMARC?

Yes. Gmail and Yahoo now require proper authentication for bulk senders, and your email tool will walk you through setting it up on your domain. See Google's email sender guidelines.

Is a newsletter platform the same as an email marketing tool?

Not quite. Newsletter platforms focus on publishing and growing an audience, and email marketing tools focus on automation and sales. See our best newsletter platforms comparison if publishing is your main goal.
