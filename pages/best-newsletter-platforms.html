How to choose the right newsletter platform

  1. Decide what the newsletter is for. If it's the product (you earn from ads or subscriptions), pick a creator platform like beehiiv, Substack or Ghost. If it supports a business, pick a tool with automation, like GetResponse, MailerLite or Kit.
  2. Check how you'll make money. Paid subscriptions, sponsors and selling your own products each point to different platforms. Compare the revenue cut, not just the monthly price.
  3. Price it at your future list size. Most platforms charge more as your list grows. Check the price at 10,000 and 50,000 subscribers, not just today's.
  4. Make sure you can leave. You should be able to export your subscribers and, ideally, keep your own domain.
  5. Test with a real issue. Write and send one real edition on the free plan before you move your whole list.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best newsletter platform in 2027?

It depends on what the newsletter is for. If the newsletter itself is your business, beehiiv has the strongest growth and monetization tools. If you want the easiest start, try Substack. If you sell digital products, look at Kit. If you run a small business that needs automation, look at GetResponse or MailerLite.

Which newsletter platform is free?

beehiiv, Substack, Kit, MailerLite and Systeme.io all have free options, and Ghost is free if you host it yourself. Free plans usually cap subscribers or features, so check each platform's limits.

Which platform takes the smallest cut of paid subscriptions?

Substack takes a percentage of paid subscription revenue. beehiiv and Ghost are reported to take 0% on their paid plans. Check each platform's current fees, because they change.

Can I move my newsletter to another platform later?

Yes. All the major platforms let you export your subscriber list, and most let you import one. Moving paid subscribers takes more work, so check each platform's migration help first.

Do I need a newsletter platform or an email marketing tool?

If you publish regular content to readers, a newsletter platform is the better fit. If you mostly send sales emails, automations and abandoned-cart flows, an email marketing tool like GetResponse or MailerLite fits better. Compare them in our best email marketing tools guide.
