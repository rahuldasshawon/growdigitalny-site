Subject: Daily Tech Brief: Bessent Pushes Nvidia for an Outside CEO (November 16, 2026)

Three stories from the last 24 hours: Washington is leaning hard on Nvidia's board, Apple is testing health AI with real users, and Amazon's satellite internet plans hit a setback. Here's what each one means for your business.

------------------------------------------------------------
1. TREASURY SECRETARY TELLS NVIDIA TO LOOK OUTSIDE FOR ITS NEXT CEO

On Sunday, November 15, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Fox News that Nvidia needs a "dramatic shakeup" and that its board should pick a CEO from outside the company. Jensen Huang resigned on Thursday, November 12, after reports about his relationship with a former executive of Singapore-based Megaspeed, and CFO Colette Kress is now interim CEO. Bessent also questioned whether the company chased chip sales that broke US export controls. Nvidia reports quarterly earnings this week.

Why it matters: Nvidia's chips sit under most of the AI tools you pay for. A long leadership fight or tighter export scrutiny could affect supply and pricing further down the line, but nothing has changed for end users yet.

What to do: Don't switch or cancel AI tools over this headline. If you're about to sign an annual AI or cloud contract, check whether it allows price changes mid-term.

Source (CNBC): https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/15/bessent-nvidia-ceo-lutnick-megaspeed.html

------------------------------------------------------------
2. APPLE STARTS A 200,000-PERSON STUDY ON HOW PEOPLE USE HEALTH AI

Apple launched the Apple Health AI Usage Study in the US on November 15, working with clinical AI company OpenEvidence. Adults 21 and over with an Apple Intelligence-capable iPhone on iOS 26 or later can ask health questions in a study app and rate the answers, which cite sources like NEJM, JAMA and Nature. Apple says it wants up to 200,000 participants and that study data won't be used to train AI models.

Why it matters: Big platforms are moving toward AI answers that cite trusted, primary sources. If you publish health, wellness or other advice content, that's the direction your visibility in AI answers is heading.

What to do: Pick your top three advice pages and add links to primary sources plus a short note on who wrote or reviewed each one.

Source (Apple Newsroom): https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/11/apple-launches-research-study-to-explore-the-use-of-ai-for-health/

------------------------------------------------------------
3. A VULCAN ROCKET FAILURE COSTS AMAZON LEO A BATCH OF SATELLITES

ULA's Vulcan rocket hit an anomaly on November 15 during the first of its Amazon Leo missions, and the 40 Leo satellites on board were lost. Amazon says it has launched nearly 400 satellites across 14 missions so far, and its next launch, 36 satellites on an Ariane 6, is set for no earlier than November 23. Amazon hasn't given a date for customer service.

Why it matters: Amazon Leo is shaping up to be the main rival to Starlink for satellite internet. Setbacks like this mean fewer options and less price competition for remote teams and rural locations, at least for now.

What to do: If you're counting on satellite internet for a remote site or team, plan around what's available today. Don't wait on Leo's timeline.

Source (Amazon): https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/innovation-at-amazon/project-kuiper-satellite-rocket-launch-progress-updates
